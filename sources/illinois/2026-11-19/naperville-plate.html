Automated License Plate Readers

The Naperville Police Department uses automated license plate reader (ALPR) technology to enhance public safety and assist officers and investigators in locating stolen vehicles, wanted persons, missing or endangered persons, and vehicles associated with criminal activity. ALPR technology can also provide investigative leads that help identify suspects and witnesses, recover stolen property, and solve crimes.

Policy

The Department's use of ALPR technology is governed by department policy (PDF). That policy establishes requirements for how the technology may be used, who may access the system, how searches are documented, how information is retained and shared, and how system use is audited. Because the policy was recently updated, the previous version of the policy (PDF) is also available for transparency and historical reference.

Successes

ALPR technology has assisted Naperville Police in locating missing and endangered people, developing investigative leads, identifying wanted individuals, and recovering stolen vehicles. Read a summary of successful uses of the technology (PDF).

Safeguards

We recognize that ALPR technology is a powerful investigative tool and that its use raises legitimate privacy considerations. For that reason, the Department has established safeguards designed to ensure that the technology is used only for legitimate law enforcement and public safety purposes.

  • Access to the ALPR system is limited to authorized and trained personnel. Historical searches must be connected to a legitimate law enforcement or public safety purpose and include identifying information such as an incident, CAD, or case number, the type of incident, and the reason for the search.
  • Searches and system activity are logged and subject to audit, allowing the Department to review who accessed the system, when it was accessed, what was searched, and the stated purpose for the search.
  • Routine ALPR data collected by Naperville is retained for 30 days and then deleted unless the information must be preserved for an authorized investigative, evidentiary, legal, or records-retention purpose.
  • Manual audits are conducted by supervisory staff.
  • ALPR information may only be accessed, used, or shared for authorized law enforcement or public safety purposes and in accordance with applicable law and Department policy.
  • Suspected misuse of the ALPR system is reviewed and investigated. Misuse may result in loss of system access, disciplinary action, criminal investigation or prosecution when applicable, and other consequences.
  • The complete policy linked above provides additional information regarding authorized uses, prohibited uses, privacy protections, data sharing, retention, hot lists, training, and auditing.

FAQs

What are Automated License Plate Readers (ALPR)?

Automated License Plate Readers (ALPRs) are high-resolution digital cameras with an infrared filter to capture images of vehicle license plates. ALPRs are designed to capture images of the vehicle’s license plate and basic characteristics, such as make, model, and color.

ALPRs are not designed to:

  • Capture occupants of vehicles or use facial recognition
  • Connect to law enforcement databases
  • Capture names, dates of birth, or social security numbers, or continuously track individuals
  • Record video
  • Monitor speed or red-light violations, or issue citations

What is ALPR data NOT used for?

ALPR data is not used for civil immigration enforcement, traffic enforcement, harassment or intimidation, targeting based on protected characteristics (such as race, sex, or religion), or any personal use. Flock ALPR does not forecast behavior or predict who will commit a crime.

How long is ALPR data stored?

Data captured by the ALPR system is retained for 30 days. After that period, it is automatically purged unless it is, or is reasonably expected to become, evidence in a criminal or civil case, or must be preserved due to a lawful order. 

Why does the Department retain ALPR information for 30 days?

Historical ALPR information can become important after a crime is reported or after investigators identify a vehicle associated with an investigation. The 30-day period provides investigators with a limited window to develop those leads while preventing routine vehicle location information from being retained indefinitely. The Department's policy requires historical searches to be connected to a documented law enforcement or public safety purpose. 

What safeguards are in place to protect citizens’ privacy?

All ALPR data is used strictly for law enforcement purposes. The data is owned by the Naperville Police Department and is never sold to private entities. Additionally, access is limited, logged and audited. 

Does an ALPR alert automatically give an officer a reason to stop a vehicle?

No. An ALPR alert is an investigative lead and does not, by itself, establish reasonable suspicion or probable cause for a traffic stop, search or arrest. Officers are required to evaluate the circumstances and verify the alert before taking enforcement action whenever reasonably practical. This is an important protection already contained in your policy. 

What happens if an employee misuses the ALPR system?

Suspected misuse is reviewed and investigated. Misuse may result in suspension or loss of ALPR access, disciplinary action, and criminal prosecution when applicable.
