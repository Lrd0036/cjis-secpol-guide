Automated License Plate Readers (ALPR)

The San Marcos Police Department uses Automated License Plate Readers (ALPR) to assist in investigations and enhance public safety.

ALPR cameras capture images of vehicles as they drive by that can then be accessed by San Marcos Police officers for up to 30 days. Since implementing these cameras, we have seen an increase in recovered stolen vehicles, and they have assisted in investigations throughout the city.


ALPR Information Sharing and Non-Disclosure Agreement

Automated License Plate Readers:

  • Capture license plate images
  • Automatically alert police of stolen vehicles and vehicles related to wanted or missing persons
  • Generate helpful leads on investigations
  • Have policies in place to ensure restricted and responsible use, including deletion of data after 30 days and regular audits
  • Have a transparency portal so you can see how we're using them
  • Maximize the efficiency of our department, saving resources, time, and money

Automated License Plate Readers Do Not:

  • Use facial recognition 
  • Capture any personal or biometric information 
  • Enforce traffic laws like speeding or running red lights
  • Track where a car has been or where it is going

San Marcos Police Department ALPR Policy

Addressing ALPR Concerns:

During the City Council meeting on December 2, 2025,  Council voted to discontinue the contract with Flock Safety, Inc.. As of February 1, 2026, all city contracted Flock cameras had been deactivated and removed. 

Effective June 9, 2025, the Police Department stopped automatic sharing of license plate reader (LPR) data with other law enforcement agencies.

  1. The Department will now share data only upon both:
    • Request
    • Confirmation of a specific criminal investigation or prosecution  
  2. The Department will require each agency complete an “ALPR Sharing and Non-Disclosure Agreement.” 
  3. Requests will be accepted for: 
    • Class B state offenses and above
    • Missing or endangered persons
    • Stolen vehicles
    • Hate crimes
    • Sex crimes
    • Be on the Lookout (BOLO) reports such as Amber and Silver Alerts

 Audits of LPR data were not mandated in the April 2022 policy but were added in the March 2025 revisions proposed by staff.  

  1. The current policy requires an audit be done monthly.  
  2. Turning off automatic sharing access with other law enforcement agencies allows the Police Department to audit its own employees to ensure all inquiries have both: 
    • A “Reason” for inquiry 
    • A “Case Number”
  3. The Department will randomize audit controls. This will be done by: 
    • Selecting thirty (30) random inquiries per month 
    • Proactively auditing selected inquiries to ensure: 
      • Lawful usage of the system per policy
      • Proper inclusion of the inquiry “Reason” and “Case number”
