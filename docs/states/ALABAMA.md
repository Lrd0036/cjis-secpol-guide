# Alabama CJIS requirements

Public sources checked **November 19, 2026**. The Alabama Justice Information Commission (AJIC) sets policy. The Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA) operates the state systems and is Alabama's CJIS Systems Agency for the FBI. The applicable state requirement often turns on who operates the system, which records it holds and who receives them. A sheriff releasing a criminal history, a state agency buying cloud hosting and a city contracting for license plate reader storage have different obligations. ## Criminal histories, access and disclosure

- **Classify the record.** Alabama's definition of criminal justice information (CJI) includes specified biometric, identity, case and other justice data, and certain data ALEA or FBI CJIS provides for hiring. Criminal history record information (CHRI) covers identifiable histories of arrests, charges and dispositions. The rule distinguishes those histories from investigative files and nonidentifying statistics. Information drawn from systems established under §41-9-621 is privileged. Someone seeking an independently open record can request it from its original custodian. [§41-9-590(9), (11)](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=41-9-590) · [265-X-2-.01(2)](https://admincode.legislature.state.al.us/api/chapter/265-X-2#page=2) · [§41-9-594.1](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=41-9-594.1)
- **Identify the requester and purpose.** A criminal justice agency can obtain direct access after completing a privacy and security agreement, for justice administration or its own pre-employment checks and assignments. A noncriminal justice agency needs qualifying legal authority, appropriate approval and a privacy and security agreement for indirect access. A research request has its own agreement, research design and director approval. The director approves network access. Additional devices may require an agreement. [265-X-2-.02(1)–(2)](https://admincode.legislature.state.al.us/api/rule/265-X-2-.02) · [265-X-2-.04](https://admincode.legislature.state.al.us/api/chapter/265-X-2#page=10)
- **Control further use.** Noncriminal justice recipients covered by the agreement must limit use, prevent further dissemination, retain required records for audit and familiarize staff with the rules. The rule also bars confirming a record's existence to an ineligible requester. Section 642 prohibits disclosure to a party with neither the Commission-determined need nor the right to know. System logs have their own release criteria. Willful false pretenses, falsification and unauthorized communication have separate statutory provisions. [265-X-2-.02(2)–(4)](https://admincode.legislature.state.al.us/api/rule/265-X-2-.02) · [§41-9-642](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=41-9-642) · [§§41-9-601–602](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=41-9-601)
- **Give the subject a correction path.** ALEA's Form 46 specifies identification, classifiable fingerprints and a $25 personal review fee. ALEA's public instructions list $5 per additional copy. The rule allows a challenge within one calendar year of the response to the CHRI application. Section 645 gives a separate 30-day circuit-court appeal from an unsatisfactory agency decision. A successful correction can require notice to others holding the record. ALEA's public pages also link older form versions. Check the issuing office's current instructions before filing. [Form 46](https://www.alea.gov/sites/default/files/2024-06/ALEA%20CJIS%20Form%2046%20-%20Application%20to%20Review-Challenge%20Alabama%20CHRI_rev.2.2_3-8-2023.pdf) · [ALEA fee instructions](https://www.alea.gov/sbi/criminal-justice-services/criminal-records/alabama-background-check) · [265-X-2-.03](https://admincode.legislature.state.al.us/api/chapter/265-X-2#page=8) · [§41-9-645](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=41-9-645) · [§41-9-646](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=41-9-646)
- **Account for special records.** Juvenile law-enforcement files carry additional access safeguards. Expungement orders require affected local records to be forwarded to ALEA and protected there, with action on federal entries where applicable. Arrest and fugitive records have separate biometric and disposition rules. The 30-day notice in §625(b) concerns the specified fugitive custodial event. Section 626 has a 24-hour forwarding rule for required identifying data, including a weekend/holiday extension and separate photograph instructions. [§12-15-134](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=12-15-134) · [§15-27-7](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=15-27-7) · [§41-9-625](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=41-9-625) · [§41-9-626](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=41-9-626)

## Background checks after October 1, 2026

**Act 2026-374 took effect October 1.** ALEA must operate the Alabama Background Check Service. AJIC sets rules, ALEA establishes procedures and ALEA can contract with vendors to perform functions on its behalf. The Legislature's November §41-9-650 display still shows the prior licensing language and explicitly directs readers to the Act. The enrolled bill strikes that text and adds the service provisions. Use the enacted text for §650 and the individual Code sections for §§650.1–650.5. A check on a person under 18 needs parental consent. [§41-9-650.1(a)–(e)](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/code-of-alabama?section=41-9-650.1) |

**Screening depends on the program.** For covered child-care personnel, DHR requires comprehensive checks before employment, at least every five years and after 180 days out of child-care employment. Its process includes FBI fingerprint and registry checks. Title 38 covers written consent and disclosures for specified care providers. Education personnel have their own screening statute. Alabama §41-9-652 names ALEA as repository and addresses the National Child Protection Act of 1993. The FBI separately tracks state participation in the 1998 National Crime Prevention and Privacy Compact. As of its July 2025 map, the FBI reported no known current ratification action for Alabama. AlaCOP publishes a 14-character password minimum and a multifactor authentication guide describing a phased rollout with agency notification. The guide gives no statewide completion date.
