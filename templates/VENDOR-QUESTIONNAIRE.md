# CJIS Vendor Questionnaire

## Instructions

Answer for the exact service, edition, deployment model, support plan, regions, and subcontractors proposed. Attach evidence. A corporate-wide “yes” does not answer a system-scoped question.

## Service and boundary

1. Identify the legal entity providing the service.
2. Name the product, edition, version, deployment model, and regions.
3. Describe every component that can process, store, transmit, display, log, back up, or administer CJI.
4. Provide current logical, network, trust-boundary, and data-flow diagrams.
5. List all support, telemetry, crash-reporting, analytics, content-delivery, backup, and recovery paths.
6. Identify all subprocessors and the functions, data, regions, and access each receives.
7. State what customer configuration is required for the proposed CJIS boundary.

## Claims and authority

8. State the exact meaning and scope of any “CJIS compliant” claim.
9. Identify every agency/jurisdiction that has accepted the same service boundary and provide releasable evidence.
10. Identify conditions, exclusions, expired decisions, and material differences from this deployment.
11. State whether the claim is self-attested, independently assessed, contractually warranted, or accepted by a CJIS authority.
12. Identify the policy version and jurisdictional supplement used for each claim.

## Agreements and oversight

13. Will the vendor and applicable personnel execute the required CJIS Security Addendum or other agreements?
14. Describe support for agency oversight, audits, inspections, evidence requests, and remediation.
15. Provide notification and approval terms for material service and subprocessor changes.
16. Describe record retention, return, portability, sanitization, and contract-termination procedures.
17. Identify any contractual limits that could prevent the agency or CJIS authority from obtaining evidence.

## People and access

18. List roles with actual or potential access to CJI, encryption keys, backups, logs, hosts, hypervisors, support consoles, or recovery systems.
19. Describe authorization, screening, training, access agreements, and periodic access review for each role.
20. Describe privileged access, just-in-time elevation, approval, session recording, emergency access, and break-glass review.
21. Identify countries and locations from which personnel can administer or support the service.
22. Describe transfer and termination controls, including maximum access-removal time.
23. Identify non-human identities and how credentials, scope, rotation, and ownership are controlled.

## Identity and access control

24. Describe federation, MFA, authenticator strength, device identity, session controls, and reauthentication.
25. Explain how tenant separation, role enforcement, least privilege, and privileged-function logging are tested.
26. Explain how access to CJI is prevented through support, observability, debugging, and administrative paths.
27. Provide recent privilege-review and access-test evidence.

## Cryptography and key custody

28. Identify encryption for CJI in transit, at rest, in backups, in queues, in logs, and on endpoints.
29. Identify cryptographic modules and provide applicable validation evidence.
30. Describe key generation, storage, access, rotation, revocation, recovery, destruction, and separation of duties.
31. State whether the vendor can decrypt customer CJI and under what workflow.
32. Describe customer-managed-key options and the functions that remain visible to the provider.

## Logging and monitoring

33. List security and CJI-relevant events logged, including privileged actions, access, queries, exports, configuration changes, and authentication events.
34. Describe time synchronization, tamper protection, retention, capacity monitoring, review, alerting, and log-failure response.
35. Explain how the agency can obtain logs without exposing another tenant's information.
36. Provide sanitized sample records and evidence of recent review and alert handling.

## Vulnerability, configuration, and development

37. Provide component inventory, secure baselines, change-control process, and unsupported-component policy.
38. Describe vulnerability discovery, scanning, penetration testing, remediation SLAs, exception handling, and customer notification.
39. Describe secure development, threat modeling, code review, dependency controls, build integrity, release approval, and security testing.
40. Provide relevant recent assessment summaries and remediation status.

## Incident response and resilience

41. Provide the incident response plan, roles, CJIS/agency notification path, reporting timeline, and evidence-preservation process.
42. Describe the last relevant exercise or incident and resulting corrective actions.
43. State recovery objectives and provide recent backup restoration and disaster-recovery test evidence.
44. Explain how recovered data retains authorization, integrity, encryption, and auditability.
45. Identify agency dependencies during containment and recovery.

## Physical, media, and maintenance

46. Describe physical security and environmental protection for every facility in scope.
47. Describe visitor management, delivery/removal controls, media inventory, transport, reuse, and sanitization.
48. Describe local and nonlocal maintenance authorization, tools, personnel, session protection, and records.

## Supply chain

49. Provide the supplier and subprocessor risk-management process.
50. Describe component provenance, integrity verification, notification agreements, inspection rights, and disposal.
51. Identify single points of failure and suppliers whose failure could expose or interrupt CJI.
